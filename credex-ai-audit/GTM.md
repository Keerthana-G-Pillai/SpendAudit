# Go-To-Market Plan

## Exact target user

Engineering managers and CTOs at post-seed to Series A SaaS startups, 10–40 employees, who personally approved AI tool subscriptions one at a time over the past 18 months and have never audited the combined total. They own the dev tools budget but have no dedicated FinOps function. They feel the monthly bill but have no benchmark to know if it is reasonable.

Secondary user: solo founders or indie hackers running small teams of 2–5 who subscribed to multiple AI tools "just to try" and forgot to cancel or downgrade.

## What they Google or scroll through right before wanting this tool

- "is cursor worth it vs github copilot"
- "chatgpt team vs plus for small team"
- "how much should a startup spend on ai tools"
- Scrolling a Twitter/X thread comparing AI coding tools
- Reading a Hacker News post titled "We cut our AI tool spend by 40%"

## Where they hang out online

- **r/SaaS** and **r/startups** on Reddit — engineering managers post cost questions here regularly
- **Hacker News** — Show HN posts and "Ask HN: how do you manage AI tool costs" threads
- **Indie Hackers community** — founders openly share spend and cost structures
- **Slack: Ramen Club, Online Geniuses, MicroConf** — startup operator communities
- **X/Twitter** — following accounts like @levelsio, @marc_louvion, @thepatwalls who share indie SaaS finance takes
- **LinkedIn** — engineering managers share "lessons learned" posts about tooling decisions

## How to get the first 100 users in 30 days with $0 budget

1. **Day 1–3: Launch on Hacker News as Show HN** — "Show HN: Free AI spend auditor for startups — finds overspend in 2 minutes." Post at 8am EST on a Tuesday or Wednesday. Respond to every comment within the first 2 hours. This alone can drive 200–500 visitors if it gets traction.

2. **Day 1–7: Post in relevant subreddits** — r/SaaS, r/startups, r/webdev. Frame as a tool you built because you personally had this problem. Authentic founder story posts get 3–5x more engagement than product announcements.

3. **Day 3–10: DM 20 founders directly on X** — Search "paying for cursor copilot claude" or "ai tools budget" on X. Find people who have publicly mentioned their AI tool stack. Send a short DM: "I built a free tool that audits AI tool spend — would you try it and give feedback?"

4. **Day 7–14: Write a short blog post** — "We audited 50 startups' AI tool spend. Here's what we found." Publish on Medium, dev.to, and Substack Notes. Share individual findings (anonymized). The data angle gets picked up and shared.

5. **Day 14–30: Get into Indie Hackers and Slack communities** — Share the blog post findings in Ramen Club Slack, Indie Hackers forum, and MicroConf community. These are warm audiences who care about unit economics.

## The unfair distribution channel

Credex already has relationships with startups who have bought discounted AI credits. Every past Credex customer is a warm lead for the audit tool — they clearly care about AI spend. A single email to the existing customer list from Credex saying "we built a free tool to audit your team's AI spend" converts far better than cold outreach because the trust is already there.

## What week-1 traction looks like if this works

- 300–500 unique visitors from HN Show HN post
- 80–120 completed audits
- 25–40 email captures (25–35% of completions)
- 5–10 shareable URLs actually visited by someone other than the creator
- 2–4 inbound consultation requests for Credex
