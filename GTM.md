# Go-To-Market Strategy

### Who exactly is the target user

The exact target user is a CTO, VP of Engineering, or FinOps Lead at a Series A or Series B startup with 30 to 150 employees. At this stage they have significant monthly API spend and seat licenses but no dedicated procurement function. They are actively looking for ways to extend runway without cutting engineering headcount. They approved AI tool subscriptions one by one over 18 months and have never audited the combined total.

Secondary user is the non-technical founder — the CEO who pays the card bill but cannot evaluate whether each tool is necessary. This profile emerged directly from user interviews conducted during the build week and shaped the plain-language framing of every recommendation in the audit engine.

### What they are Googling right before they want this

They are searching for "Cursor vs GitHub Copilot enterprise pricing", "how to reduce Anthropic API bill", "ChatGPT Team vs Enterprise plan limits", and "are AI coding tools worth the seat cost." Alternatively they are scrolling their monthly Stripe invoice and realising their AI tool line item has quietly become a top three SaaS expense with no single person owning the decision.

### Where they hang out online

On Reddit they are in r/SaaS, r/startups, and r/devops. On Slack they are in Y Combinator alumni networks, the IndieHackers community, and FinOps-focused groups inside the Cloud Native Computing Foundation Slack. On X they follow Build in Public founders and VC partners at firms like a16z and Sequoia who tweet actively about AI margins and portfolio burn rates.

### How to get the first 100 users in 30 days with zero paid budget

The launch anchors on three moves executed in sequence. First a Show HN post titled "Show HN: Free tool that tells you exactly how much you're overpaying for AI tools" — Hacker News responds strongly to cost-saving utilities and the no-login-required format maximises completion rate. Second cold outreach to partners at 10 to 15 Seed and Series A funds with a single line pitch: send this to your portfolio founders and it will instantly show them if they are bleeding runway on redundant AI subscriptions. VCs are motivated to reduce portfolio burn and act as distribution nodes forwarding to dozens of target users simultaneously at zero cost. Third seeding Twitter with real scorecard screenshots — posting audit results showing specific dollar savings with the shareable URL triggers the native viral loop built into the product.

### The unfair distribution channel

Credex's unfair advantage is actual supply of discounted institutional credits. Anyone can build a spend calculator. Only Credex can put a button at the end of the audit that says click here to claim these exact API credits at 30% off today. This converts a diagnostic tool into a direct transaction and makes the CTA on the results page uniquely credible rather than a generic upsell.

### What week one traction looks like if this works

1,500 unique visitors driven primarily by the HN launch and VC forwarding. 600 completed audits at a 40% completion rate enabled by the no upfront email gate policy. 120 leads captured at 20% post-audit email conversion. 15 high-intent consultation conversations generated for the Credex sales team in the first seven days.
